# Quotation and source standard

Explicit user requirement, 2026-09-10: include Mishnah, Talmud/Gemara, Kabbalah, Septuagint, extracanonical books, scrolls and other relevant information. Every factual statement must be backed by a real quotation.

## Evidence record

For each publishable claim record:
- Claim ID and exact proposed wording in each language.
- Short verbatim quotation sufficient to support it, preserving the original wording. Mark omissions; never join fragments into an apparently continuous sentence.
- Work, precise locator, edition or manuscript identifier, source language and translator when relevant.
- Link to the inspected passage or scan, inspection date, and verification status. A search snippet or AI summary is a discovery aid, not verification.
- What the quotation establishes and what it does not; identify speaker, literary context and interpretive uncertainty.

Distinguish quotation from paraphrase typographically. Original text and an assistant-produced translation must be separate, with the latter labeled “our translation” / “תרגום שלנו”. Never present a translation back into Hebrew as an ancient Hebrew original. Identify quoted published translations and respect their quotation limits; use concise supporting excerpts rather than reproducing large passages.

A quotation establishes that a text says something; it does not automatically establish the historicity of its narrative. Attribute claims accordingly. Dating, authorship and archaeology need their own verified scholarly evidence and quotations, not just an ancient narrative. A traditional attribution and a scholarly dating may be presented together with separate attributions and evidence.

## Locators by collection

| Collection | Minimum useful locator |
|---|---|
| Tanakh | Book, chapter, verse(s), textual version |
| Mishnah | Tractate, chapter, mishnah |
| Babylonian Talmud | Tractate, folio and side, and passage opening when helpful; identify Gemara, Mishnah or later commentary |
| Jerusalem Talmud | Tractate, chapter/halakhah and edition-specific page/column or other stable locator |
| Kabbalah | Named work, section, volume/folio/page and edition; use the work's actual reference system |
| Septuagint | Book, chapter/verse in the named Greek edition; note numbering differences from Hebrew; label any translation |
| Extracanonical works | Exact work title and version, chapter/section/verse and edition; avoid ambiguous “Ezra” or “Enoch” labels |
| Dead Sea Scrolls | Manuscript siglum, fragment, column and line(s), edition; preserve gaps and editorial restorations |
| Biblical megillot | Book/chapter/verse; if discussing a particular scroll, add manuscript identity |
| Scholarly or archaeological material | Author/institution, work or object identifier, page/section/line and stable link |

Do not presume “external books” means the same collection in all religious canons. Do not treat medieval commentary, rabbinic literature or Kabbalah as contemporary evidence for an earlier event without argument.

## Comparisons, calculations and missing evidence

Quote both sides of a textual comparison. Align the same unit, event or category before claiming disagreement. Explain when numbering, translation choice, recension or manuscript damage affects comparison.

For a calculation, quote every input passage, give the values and operation, and label the result “our calculation”. The computed result is not a source quotation. Illustrations and maps derived from the text likewise need labeled assumptions and quoted source inputs.

Negative universal claims cannot normally be established by one quotation. Prefer a bounded positive statement such as “this inventory lists these items” supported by its text. If absence matters, identify the exact corpus searched and method, narrow the claim to that corpus, and avoid claiming absence proves an object's fate.

For disputed matters, quote and attribute the relevant positions. Do not fabricate balance or manufacture an explanation where none was verified. If access is unavailable, seek a reputable accessible edition; if none is available, exclude the claim from publication and state the gap.

## Presenting evidence in short formats

In HTML: place a short quote and locator beside the claim, with expandable context and a source link.
In a carousel: place short evidence excerpts with readable locators on the relevant slides; put fuller context and links in the companion source sheet/caption.
In video: display the key supporting excerpt legibly and a concise locator; provide the full claim ledger and additional excerpts in the caption or companion page. Do not pretend an unreadable footnote satisfies the requirement.

Before calling a package ready for publication, check that every factual claim in hooks, voiceover, labels, maps, captions and explanatory notes maps to a verified evidence record. Editorial proposals and production choices should be labeled as proposals; they are not historical claims.
